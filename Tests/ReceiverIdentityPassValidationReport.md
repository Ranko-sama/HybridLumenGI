# Receiver identity recorder validation

This report covers `HybridLumenGIReceiverIdentityPass.cs`, independently of the identity shader's numerical/DXC tests and the CPU identity-frame builder tests.

`powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File Tests/Validate-ReceiverIdentityPass.ps1` passed **17 scenarios / 439 checks** on 2026-10-27, under both Windows PowerShell 5.1 and PowerShell 7, and again in the integration-13 unified run. The harness compiles the actual recorder class. Unity, RenderGraph, identity-frame inputs and fence completion are explicit recording substitutes. It does not execute Unity, a GPU, a shader, a native graphics fence or RenderGraph's real resource compiler.

Checked recording contracts:

- Four declared stages: clear/upload, unknown-rectangle masking, raster claims, resolve. Candidate buffers are full-resolution 12-byte `uint3`; object rows retain holes and are 80 bytes, with one zero dummy allocation when logical count is zero. Native resource handle conversions happen only inside callbacks.
- The capture stage reads both original camera depth and material-capture depth, writes an independent RGBA8 dummy RT, and uses pixel UAV slot 1. Every draw has a distinct MPB, integer owner index, frozen alpha inputs and explicit matrices; the common private material uses the source Cull pass. Irrelevant alpha textures bind white. Source materials/renderers are never modified.
- Fresh-source validation occurs immediately before capture. False/throwing validation, initialization failure, partial draw failure and exhausted retirement capacity result in rejected receipts and resolve count zero. A resolve-command failure rejects the receipt even when output contents cannot be guaranteed; the caller must independently gate history on this receipt.
- Empty frames, row holes, clamped/empty unknown rectangles, multiple recorder instances, missing resources/capabilities and partial graph construction are covered.
- A successful receipt follows resolve recording, not capture alone; it is not GPU completion. Duplicate resolve callbacks do not replay the receipt.
- Private MPBs/materials remain retained until their fence passes. Disposal before capture cancels future draws; disposal between capture and resolve retains the material until a later recorded fence completes. Resolve failure still inserts a retirement fence after attempted draws. Fence creation/read failure never permits destruction.
- At most 32 pending leases per material owner and 16 global material owners, including quarantined owners. Exhaustion rejects identity capture. Abandoned, never-GPU-used leases are canceled across engine frames by the next Record or end-context callback; late callbacks cannot resurrect them. Actual GPU-attempted leases without a completion proof are never reclaimed by age.
- Optional timing observers surround each complete stage with local begin/end callbacks. They are armed only after all four passes are constructed. Partial construction, disposed or late state, duplicate and out-of-order stages reject timing without fabricating a complete sample. A valid empty frame or source-rejected draw still closes its normal stage pair.
- Observer exceptions invalidate timing at most once without setting identity failure or changing its receipt. A throwing begin still offers the same stage's end cleanup; the per-frame timing helper decides whether a real marker was begun. Real draw/resolve failures retain their existing identity fallback behavior.
- Clear explicitly reads material-capture depth, establishing capture-before-clear ordering. Candidate-buffer and identity-output dependencies chain the remaining stages. Resolve permits global-state modification for the optional native end event; its observer ends after dispatch/fence, receipt notification and retirement collection. These declarations are checked by recording substitutes, not Unity's scheduler.

The runtime source bound by the unified report has SHA-256 `CFD2B3566A1ACBE446C483689CC527EE65C41037F2D5FA9FA3C27E53D6FB56F9`. Provider token handling, native ABI1 intervals, Recorder 3/7 scopes and command-emission fault isolation are covered separately by [GpuFrameTimingValidationReport.md](GpuFrameTimingValidationReport.md).

RenderGraph owns all transient buffers and the dummy RT; the recorder never releases them. Fences protect only privately owned Material/MPB resources. One capture's candidate+dummy payload is `16 × fullWidth × fullHeight` bytes, plus `80 × max(1,rowCount)` object bytes. History identity textures belong to the caller and are additional. These are logical allocation estimates, not measured driver allocation.

Remaining target checks: Unity shader importer and DX12 UAV register mapping; real depth/alpha agreement with stock Lit; barriers between raster UAV writes and resolve reads; graphics-fence behavior, late/abandoned graph handling and lifecycle on real renderer/device changes. The caller additionally rejects unsupported MSAA, projection/viewport/debug/alpha-to-coverage states and validates sources again inside the capture callback. Passing this harness does not establish production support for those cases.
